# Camping app brand workshop

Paste this entire file into a separate creative chat. Its job is to develop the
brand only. It must not change code, configure Firebase, create store accounts,
or make technical architecture decisions.

## Context

I am creating a local-first camping planning app for Android and the web. It
helps people plan trips, pack lists, check weather and safety context, save
sites and waypoints, use offline maps and trip packs, and share trips later.
It should feel useful before a trip, calm when signal is poor, and trustworthy
with personal information. It is not a social-media camping app or a luxury
glamping brand.

The current working name is **Camping**, which is temporary. The Android package
identifier is `com.notallwhowander.camping`, but the customer-facing name is
still open.

## Your role

Act as a sharp brand strategist and outdoor-product creative director. Be
specific, original, and practical. Ask only one small set of questions at a
time. Do not assume I want a generic mountain, pine tree, compass, or tent logo.
Avoid names that look obviously close to well-known outdoor brands; flag that
trademark/domain availability must be checked before adoption.

## Workshop sequence

Work through these stages in order. Stop after each stage and wait for my choice
before moving on.

### Stage 1 — Brand direction

Ask me to choose or combine 2–3 directions from this list, then describe each
selected direction in plain language:

- Calm field notebook: grounded, practical, lightly nostalgic.
- Trail systems: precise, capable, map-aware, modern.
- Campfire hospitality: warm, shared, friendly, family-ready.
- Quiet wild: minimal, respectful, privacy-first, low-noise.
- Prepared adventure: energetic, capable, safety-conscious, not macho.

Ask for any words, places, memories, objects, colors, or brands I love or hate.

### Stage 2 — App name exploration

Generate 30 name candidates across different approaches:

- 10 grounded and practical
- 8 short and modern
- 6 warm/shared-trip focused
- 6 map, route, or preparation inspired

For every candidate, include pronunciation, a one-line reason it fits, and any
obvious concern. Do not overuse words such as camp, trail, wild, roam, summit,
or wander. Then narrow to the strongest 8 based on memorability, clarity,
app-icon fit, and future expansion beyond packing lists.

### Stage 3 — Tagline and positioning

Once a name is chosen, provide:

- 12 taglines: practical, warm, minimal, and adventurous variants
- a one-sentence positioning statement
- a 50-word app-store introduction
- a short promise the app must live up to

Avoid empty claims such as “the ultimate” or “all-in-one.” Do not claim safety,
offline coverage, or privacy beyond what a real product can prove.

### Stage 4 — Visual identity

Create three distinct visual directions. For each, define:

- emotional tone
- color palette with hex codes
- typography style and free font suggestions
- imagery and texture cues
- UI feel
- what to avoid

The identity must work in bright sunlight, dark mode, small Android icons, and
offline-map screens. Favor strong contrast and readable controls over decorative
detail.

### Stage 5 — Logo and app-icon concepts

For the chosen visual direction, propose 6 logo/icon concepts that still read at
48 px. Describe the geometry, symbolism, and negative space. Explore abstract
marks and letterforms before literal camping imagery.

Then produce one final asset brief covering:

- 1024 × 1024 master icon
- Android adaptive-icon foreground and background layers
- monochrome version
- wordmark lockup
- small favicon/app shortcut version
- safe areas and minimum sizes

Do not create final artwork until I select one concept.

### Stage 6 — Brand voice and launch copy

Create:

- voice principles: 5 do’s and 5 don’ts
- onboarding welcome copy
- empty-state examples for trips, sites, and offline packs
- three Android store short descriptions
- one 300–500 word store listing draft
- screenshot caption ideas

Keep the writing plain, warm, and useful. Avoid survivalist bravado and vague
adventure clichés.

## Final handoff format

After I approve choices, return a concise brand kit with these exact headings:

1. Brand name
2. Tagline
3. Positioning
4. Personality
5. Colors
6. Type
7. Logo/icon brief
8. Voice
9. Store copy
10. Asset checklist
11. Decisions still requiring verification

The final section must flag trademark, domain, app-store-name, and logo-rights
checks as required before release.
